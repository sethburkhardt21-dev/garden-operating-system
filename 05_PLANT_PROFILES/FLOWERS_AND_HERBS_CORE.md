# Herbs

## Basil
**Wants:** full sun, warmth, even moisture, regular harvest.  
**Plant:** after frost danger when nights are warm; succession sowing keeps young plants coming.  
**Harvest:** pinch stems above leaf nodes; remove flower buds when leaf production is the priority.  
**Watch:** downy mildew, cold damage, slug/snail injury on young plants.  
**Default placement:** K08, close to daily harvest route.  
**Biggest mistake:** waiting too long between harvests so plants become tall, flowering, and sparse.

## Rosemary
**Wants:** strong sun, sharp drainage, less frequent watering than basil/parsley.  
**Winter:** Richmond sits near a 6b–7a transition at coarse scale, so treat rosemary as conditionally hardy unless exact cultivar/microclimate proves reliable. Container culture or deliberate overwintering remains conservative.  
**Default placement:** P03 dry-herb garden.  
**Harvest:** clip tips without stripping the plant.  
**Watch:** root rot from wet soil, weak indoor light if overwintered.  
**Biggest mistake:** putting it on the same frequent-water circuit as thirsty vegetables.

## Thyme
**Wants:** full sun, drainage, lean-to-moderate fertility.  
**Habit:** low perennial.  
**Default placement:** P03.  
**Harvest:** clip young stems regularly.  
**Watch:** crown/root decline in soggy soil.  
**Biggest mistake:** smothering it under aggressive neighbors or wet mulch against the crown.

## Parsley
**Wants:** sun to part sun, even moisture, fertile soil.  
**Type:** flat-leaf Italian is leading culinary candidate unless aesthetics favor curly.  
**Default placement:** K08.  
**Harvest:** remove outer stems at base while center continues growing.  
**Watch:** swallowtail caterpillars may use parsley; decide intentionally whether some plants support them.  
**Biggest mistake:** assuming caterpillars automatically require treatment.

## Cilantro
**Wants:** cooler conditions than basil, sun/part sun, even moisture.  
**Strategy:** succession sow small batches because Richmond heat accelerates bolting.  
**Default placement:** K08, with greenhouse/shoulder-season use only when helpful.  
**Harvest:** young leaves frequently; allow selected plants to seed if coriander/beneficial habitat is desired.  
**Biggest mistake:** planting one giant batch in warm weather and expecting months of leaf harvest.

## Lavender
**Wants:** full sun, excellent drainage, airflow, relatively lean conditions.  
**Type:** cold-hardy English lavender is the default direction.  
**Default placement:** P03 dry-herb garden.  
**Water:** establishment water, then avoid chronic wetness.  
**Pruning:** shape without cutting brutally into old woody structure.  
**Biggest mistake:** treating it like a rich, frequently irrigated vegetable.

# Roses

## Classic Red Roses
**Design invariant:** red is the dominant rose color.  
**Wants:** full sun, good airflow, deep root-zone watering, strong disease performance.  
**Kentucky priority:** black spot is a major regional disease; rose rosette is a separate diagnostic risk.  
**Default architecture:** matched red entrance pair + one red transition focal; total 3–5 red roses.  
**Maintenance:** deadhead as cultivar benefits, remove damaged/diseased material, prune for structure/air movement.  
**Watch:** black spot, rose rosette symptoms, Japanese beetles, aphids, cane injury.  
**Biggest mistake:** choosing only from bloom photos while ignoring disease evidence and mature size.

Historical Kentucky resistance lists make cultivars such as `Mister Lincoln` and `Olympiad` candidates for re-check, not guarantees. See `46_ROSE_AND_FLOWER_DESIGN.md`.

## Optional White Rose
Same biological care standard. Design rule remains strict: **maximum one white rose, backyard/interior only, and never part of the symmetrical entrance pair.**

# Flowers

## Dahlias
**Wants:** full sun, fertile drained soil, consistent water, airflow.  
**Support:** stake tall/heavy-flowered cultivars early.  
**Bloom management:** cut/deadhead regularly.  
**Winter:** assume tubers are lifted/stored unless actual site/cultivar evidence proves otherwise.  
**Watch:** slugs/snails, spider mites, stem breakage, storage rot.  
**Default placement:** FL01, 8–12 plants.  
**Biggest mistake:** waiting until stems flop before support.

## Zinnias
**Wants:** full sun, warmth, airflow.  
**Harvest:** cut frequently; deadheading supports continued bloom.  
**Water:** root-zone irrigation preferred.  
**Watch:** powdery mildew, especially late/crowded.  
**Default placement:** FL02, roughly 24–36 plants across one/two successions.  
**Biggest mistake:** crowding into a mildew wall.

## Marigolds
**Wants:** sun, moderate fertility, moderate water.  
**Use:** repeated front-edge groups/punctuation, not random folklore companion placement.  
**Default amount:** roughly 18–30 across repeated edge groups.  
**Biggest mistake:** assuming marigolds magically repel every vegetable pest.

## Daisies — exact species unresolved
Shasta-daisy-type perennial is the current design placeholder, not a lock.

Selection criteria:
- Richmond hardiness
- mature height
- bloom season
- disease resistance
- spread
- cut-flower value

## Purple Coneflowers
**Wants:** sun to part sun, drained soil, moderate establishment water.  
**Use:** perennial pollinator backbone and late-summer color.  
**Default placement:** FL03, 5–7 plants.  
**Deadheading choice:** tidy/rebloom vs leaving selected seed heads for wildlife/winter structure.  
**Biggest mistake:** removing every seed head automatically.

## Black-eyed Susans
**Wants:** full sun, drained soil, moderate establishment water.  
**Use:** golden mass paired with coneflowers.  
**Default placement:** FL03, 7–9 plants.  
**Watch:** leaf spots on crowded/wet foliage, self-seeding/spread depending species.  
**Biggest mistake:** isolated single specimens instead of repeated mass.

## Cosmos
**Wants:** full sun, good drainage, not excessive fertilizer.  
**Habit:** airy/tall annual; may need support in exposed wind.  
**Default placement:** FL02, roughly 10–16 plants.  
**Bloom:** cut/deadhead for continued flowering.  
**Biggest mistake:** overfeeding into floppy foliage.

## Impatiens
**Wants:** part shade/shade, even moisture, protection from punishing afternoon sun unless cultivar specifically tolerates it.  
**Use:** one actual shade mass, not a forced full-sun symmetry feature.  
**Default:** 12–18 plants only after a real shade pocket is identified.  
**Watch:** drought wilt, downy mildew depending type, slug/snail damage.  
**Biggest mistake:** forcing them into the full-sun flower garden.

## Morning Glories
**Wants:** sun, dedicated vertical support, warm season.  
**Growth:** vigorous vine; train deliberately.  
**Risk:** aggressive self-seeding depending species/cultivar.  
**Default:** one separate arch/obelisk/trellis, 2–4 vines depending vigor.  
**Biggest mistake:** allowing vines onto tomatoes, roses, gutters, greenhouse vents, or utilities.

## Sweet Peas
**Wants:** cool-season conditions, support/netting, cool roots where possible.  
**Timing:** fundamentally different from morning glory.  
**Default:** one dedicated 8–12 ft support.  
**Harvest:** frequent cutting.  
**Warning:** ornamental sweet peas are not edible garden peas.  
**Biggest mistake:** treating them as a hot-summer vine.

## Snapdragons
**Wants:** sun, cool-season conditions, drained fertile soil.  
**Use:** spring/fall vertical spikes and cut flowers.  
**Default:** roughly 18–24 plants in grouped rows.  
**Management:** tall types may need support.  
**Biggest mistake:** expecting peak bloom through Kentucky's hottest weather.

## Lupines
**Status:** CONDITIONAL TRIAL.  
**Reason:** species/cultivar heat/humidity tolerance must be verified for Richmond.  
**Default trial:** 3–5 plants, excellent drainage.  
**Biggest mistake:** assuming cool-climate show cultivars will persist in humid Kentucky summers.

## Geraniums — exact genus unresolved
Possible meanings:
- annual bedding `Pelargonium`
- hardy perennial `Geranium`

If `Pelargonium`:
- 6–9 plants/containers as seasonal color
- protect from frost
- greenhouse staging/overwintering experiment optional

If hardy `Geranium`:
- 3–5 plants in FL03/perennial edge
- choose by mature spread/sun tolerance

## Daylilies
**Wants:** sun to part sun, ordinary drained soil, moderate water.  
**Use:** durable perennial structure/repetition.  
**Default:** 5–7 plants/clumps in FL03.  
**Maintenance:** remove spent scapes as desired; divide when congested.  
**Watch:** rust/leaf streak depending cultivar/conditions.  
**Biggest mistake:** allowing expanding clumps to smother smaller plants.

# Flower architecture rules

## Height gradient
**Back/tall:** dahlias, cosmos, tall zinnias, snapdragons, trellised vines.  
**Middle:** coneflower, black-eyed Susan, daisies, daylilies.  
**Front:** marigolds, lower geraniums, compact annuals.

## Seasonal rhythm
**Cool season:** sweet peas + snapdragons.  
**Early/mid warm season:** roses + daylilies + annual establishment.  
**High summer:** zinnia + dahlia + cosmos + marigold + coneflower/Rudbeckia.  
**Late summer/fall:** dahlia + zinnia + coneflower + Rudbeckia + selected cosmos + reblooming roses.

## Quarter-acre style invariant
Repetition beats “one of everything.” The flowers should read as clear masses from the entrance while staying compact enough for routine cutting and care.

The definitive composition is in `46_ROSE_AND_FLOWER_DESIGN.md`.
