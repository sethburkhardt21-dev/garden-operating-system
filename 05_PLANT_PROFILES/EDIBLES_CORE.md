## Heirloom Tomato
**Wants:** full sun, warm soil, fertile well-drained soil, even moisture, strong support, airflow.  
**Plant:** after frost danger and when soil/weather are reliably warm. Start indoors well before transplanting.  
**Space:** do not crowd; mature indeterminate plants need substantial width and vertical support.  
**Water:** deep and consistent; avoid repeated drought/flood swings.  
**Feed:** soil-test-driven fertility; avoid excessive nitrogen that produces foliage at expense of fruit.  
**Support:** stake/cage/trellis at planting.  
**Healthy:** vigorous green growth, flowering, fruit set, lower leaves not rapidly spotting/yellowing.  
**Watch:** early blight, Septoria, hornworms, cracking, blossom-end rot.  
**Harvest:** when fruit reaches intended mature color/feel; protect from splitting after heavy rain.  
**Biggest mistake:** planting for flavor alone without disease/airflow planning.

### Deep notes
Heirloom cultivars are retained because they are part of the requested garden. They should be balanced by resistant hybrids elsewhere so the tomato harvest is resilient. Mulch after soil warms to reduce splash and moisture fluctuation. Train foliage so harvest and disease inspection remain easy.

## Beefsteak Tomato
**Wants:** same core conditions as other tomatoes but stronger support and a long warm season.  
**Support:** heavy cage/stake; large fruit can overload weak stems.  
**Harvest:** full-size fruit with cultivar-appropriate color; pick before cracking if a major rain event follows dry weather.  
**Watch:** foliar disease + fruit cracking + blossom-end rot.  
**Biggest mistake:** using a flimsy cage and allowing an oversized canopy to collapse.

---

## Cherry Tomato
**Wants:** full sun, even water, support, regular harvest.  
**Habit:** many good cultivars are extremely vigorous indeterminate vines.  
**Harvest:** often; ripe fruit left too long splits or drops.  
**Best role:** dependable high-volume tomato and “snack harvest” crop.  
**Biggest mistake:** underestimating mature size.

## Jalapeño
**Wants:** hot sunny conditions, warm soil, even moisture, fertile but not over-rich soil.  
**Plant:** transplant after frost when temperatures are settled.  
**Support:** optional small stake if fruit load is heavy.  
**Harvest:** green for classic jalapeño use or allow to ripen red for sweeter/deeper flavor.  
**Watch:** bacterial spot, aphids, sunscald, blossom drop during temperature extremes.  
**Biggest mistake:** planting into cold spring soil and expecting rapid growth.

---

## Bell Pepper
**Wants:** warmth, full sun, steady moisture, disease-resistant cultivar.  
**Harvest:** green at full size or allow to mature to cultivar color for sweetness.  
**Support:** small cage/stake is useful where storms or heavy fruit occur.  
**Watch:** bacterial spot, sunscald, blossom-end rot-like disorders, aphids.  
**Biggest mistake:** crowding foliage so fruit stays shaded poorly yet air circulation is weak.

---

## Eggplant
**Wants:** the warmest productive garden area, full sun, consistent moisture.  
**Plant:** only after cold stress risk is low.  
**Support:** stake if cultivar has large fruit.  
**Harvest:** while skin is glossy and fruit is firm; overmature fruit becomes seedy/tough.  
**Watch:** flea beetles on young plants, Colorado potato beetle, wilt/leaf spots.  
**Biggest mistake:** putting it into a marginal cool/shady position because tomatoes got the best bed.

## Squash — exact type unresolved
**Wants:** full sun, warmth, rich well-drained soil, room.  
**Water:** consistent, especially during flowering/fruit enlargement.  
**Pollination:** male flowers often appear before female flowers; brief early periods with flowers but no fruit are normal.  
**Watch:** squash bugs, cucumber beetles, vine borers, powdery/downy mildew.  
**Biggest mistake:** not deciding whether this is bush summer squash or vining winter squash before layout.

### If summer squash
Harvest young and frequently. One missed zucchini can become enormous quickly and reduce quality.

### If winter squash
Plan vine direction and storage harvest; do not harvest before mature rind/stem cues.

## English Cucumber
**Wants:** full sun, warmth, even moisture, high fertility, excellent trellis.  
**Support:** vertical trellis is the default architecture.  
**Harvest:** frequently at cultivar size before fruit becomes overmature.  
**Watch:** cucumber beetles, bacterial wilt, powdery/downy mildew, misshapen fruit from pollination/water stress.  
**Biggest mistake:** letting vines sprawl into paths and then stepping on them.

---

## Asparagus
**Wants:** permanent full-sun, well-drained bed; weed control; long-term commitment.  
**Planting material:** crowns/transplants preferred.  
**Harvest:** none first year; limited second year; normal harvest begins around year three. UK guidance describes a mature 6–8 week harvest season.  
**After harvest:** allow fern growth to remain and recharge crowns.  
**Longevity:** a well-maintained planting can remain productive about 15 years.  
**Watch:** wet soil/root disease, weeds, weak fern growth.  
**Biggest mistake:** harvesting too aggressively during establishment or cutting healthy fern growth too early.

## Garlic
**Wants:** full sun, well-drained fertile soil, fall planting in Kentucky.  
**Planting material:** clean seed garlic/cloves.  
**Provisional timing:** UK guidance places principal home-garden planting in October to early November.  
**Plant:** pointed end up; mulch after planting as appropriate.  
**Spring:** maintain weed-free bed and even moisture during active growth.  
**Harvest:** when lower leaves have browned but enough wrappers remain to protect bulbs; cure before storage.  
**Hardneck bonus:** harvest scapes when ready.  
**Biggest mistake:** using wet poorly drained soil or anonymous grocery-store garlic as the default stock.

## Baby Potatoes
**Meaning:** “baby” describes harvest size, not cultivar.  
**Wants:** full sun, loose well-drained soil, even moisture, hilling or container depth.  
**Planting material:** certified seed potatoes.  
**Harvest:** sample young tubers after flowering/appropriate maturity for “baby” potatoes; allow remaining crop to mature longer if desired.  
**Watch:** Colorado potato beetle, foliar disease, tuber greening from light exposure.  
**Biggest mistake:** failing to hill/cover developing tubers.

---

## Golden Potatoes
**Wants:** same core management as other Irish potatoes.  
**Cultivar:** yellow/gold cultivar not yet locked.  
**Harvest:** new potatoes young; mature crop after vines naturally decline according to cultivar.  
**Storage:** cure/dry skins appropriately and store dark to prevent greening.  
**Biggest mistake:** exposing tubers to light or keeping them constantly waterlogged.

---

## Pumpkin
**Wants:** huge sunlight budget, warm soil, water, space, pollination.  
**Layout:** route vines along an exterior edge or dedicated run before planting.  
**Support:** ground culture preferred for large-fruited types; heavy trellising only for appropriately sized cultivars with fruit support.  
**Harvest:** mature rind/color + drying/corking stem cues; cut with stem attached.  
**Watch:** squash bugs, vine borers, cucumber beetles, powdery/downy mildew, black rot.  
**Storage:** many mature pumpkins can store for weeks/months under proper conditions.  
**Biggest mistake:** choosing a giant/sprawling cultivar without spatial budget.

---

## Cabbage
**Wants:** cool-season conditions, full sun, consistent water, fertile soil.  
**Season:** spring and fall windows are usually more suitable than peak Kentucky summer.  
**Protection:** insect netting/row cover can be extremely high-value against caterpillars/flea beetles when installed correctly.  
**Harvest:** firm heads at cultivar size before splitting.  
**Watch:** cabbageworms/loopers, flea beetles, black rot, heat stress.  
**Biggest mistake:** treating it like a heat-loving summer vegetable.

---

## Rhubarb
**Wants:** permanent well-drained bed, sun to part sun, cool-season establishment.  
**Kentucky varieties:** Canada Red, MacDonald, Valentine, Victoria are currently recommended by UK Extension.  
**Plant:** crowns in early spring when soil can be worked.  
**Establishment:** do not heavily harvest young crowns. UK guidance supports a full 8–10 week harvest in the third year.  
**Harvest:** pull stalks; do not eat leaves.  
**Watch:** crown rot, Japanese beetles, weak crowded crowns.  
**Biggest mistake:** eating leaves or placing crowns in chronically wet soil.

# Shared edible-garden rules

1. **Full sun is the default production target.** UK home-garden guidance recommends about eight hours where practical.
2. **Water deeply, not shallowly every day.** During Kentucky summer, target meaningful root-zone wetting rather than a damp surface.
3. **Record cultivar performance.** Disease resistance on paper is not a substitute for observing this exact garden.
4. **Keep families visible.** Tomatoes/peppers/eggplant/potatoes are Solanaceae; cucumber/squash/pumpkin are Cucurbitaceae.
5. **Do not overplant.** A smaller garden that can be maintained outperforms an oversized neglected one.
6. **Harvest on time.** Delayed harvest reduces quality and can reduce continued production.
