# Style Points — Quarter-Acre Richmond Garden

**Design goal:** abundant, productive, romantic, and intentional without turning the whole two-acre yard into landscaping.

The actual garden is ~1/4 acre. Style therefore focuses on one coherent garden campus with a greenhouse as an architectural anchor and normal yard around it.

## Core aesthetic

**Formal bones + cottage abundance**

Formal bones:
- clear paths
- repeated trellis/support materials
- greenhouse/work hub
- crisp bed edges
- deliberate red-rose focal points

Cottage abundance:
- vegetables visibly productive
- cut flowers overflowing within boundaries
- herbs near paths
- pollinator flowers layered through the season
- vines used vertically

## ESSENTIAL

- one consistent material language for major supports
- defined quarter-acre garden edge
- clean primary paths
- deliberate mulch
- legible labels
- tidy hose/irrigation routes
- repeated flower groups rather than one-of-each scattering
- greenhouse entrance kept attractive and functional
- compost/work materials given an intentional home

## POLISHED

- greenhouse centered on a useful garden-entry/work composition
- coordinated greenhouse, trellis, edging, and gate materials
- matching tomato supports
- elegant cucumber trellis
- one ornamental arch/obelisk
- 3–5 red roses used as structural punctuation
- one dedicated cutting-flower area
- small bench/rest point
- harvest/potting surface near greenhouse
- screened compost/material bay
- low-voltage path lighting if desired

## GREENHOUSE STYLE ROLE

The 16 × 24 greenhouse should look like it belongs to the garden rather than being dropped in as a utility shed.

Use:
- a straight, legible approach path
- symmetrical or balanced planting near the entrance where biology permits
- low plants near glazing so light is not blocked
- matching metal/wood accents with trellises
- gutters/downspouts routed cleanly
- potting/utility clutter hidden from the main view but immediately accessible

Avoid:
- dense shrubs against glazing
- tall flowers shading vents
- decorative screens blocking airflow
- path geometry that makes carts turn awkwardly at the door

## Garden-room sequence

A good quarter-acre walk can be:
1. garden entrance
2. greenhouse/work hub
3. kitchen garden
4. red-rose / flower transition
5. permanent edible + herb zone
6. cutting flower garden
7. flex/sprawling crop edge
8. wildflower tub / bench detail

This feels rich without needing to use the remaining 1.75 acres.

## Rose rhythm

Red roses stay primary.

Good uses:
- paired or grouped near garden entrance
- one path-end focal point
- one transition marker near flower garden

Optional white rose remains one backyard-only exception.

## Path hierarchy

### Primary path
~4 ft where practical, connecting entrance/greenhouse/kitchen garden/work zones.

### Secondary paths
~2.5–3 ft where practical.

Paths should make the quarter-acre garden readable from the house and easy to maintain in wet weather.

## Lighting

Optional warm low-voltage lighting at:
- greenhouse entrance
- main path
- one rose/arch focal point
- potting/harvest area

Keep it restrained.

## Seasonal bouquet architecture

- cool season: sweet peas + snapdragons
- early/mid warm season: roses + first zinnias
- midsummer: zinnias + cosmos + dahlias
- late summer/fall: dahlias + zinnias + coneflower + black-eyed Susan where suitable

The greenhouse can support earlier starts and propagation but should not force unnaturally early outdoor bloom schedules.

## Hard style rule

No aesthetic feature may materially reduce:
- sunlight
- greenhouse ventilation
- airflow
- irrigation access
- drainage
- harvest access
- wheelbarrow/cart access

## Scale rule

Do not spread style features across the whole two-acre property just to make the garden feel larger. The contrast between the finished quarter-acre garden and ordinary open yard is part of the design.
