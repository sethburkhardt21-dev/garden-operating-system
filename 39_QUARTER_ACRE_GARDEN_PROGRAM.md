# Quarter-Acre Garden Program — Richmond, Kentucky

**Status:** FOOTPRINT LOCKED / INTERNAL COORDINATES PROVISIONAL  
**Updated:** 2026-09-05

## Locked scale

The property remains approximately **2 acres**, but the actual cultivated Garden Operating System footprint is now approximately **1/4 acre**.

**Quarter acre = 10,890 sq ft.**

The rest of the yard remains normal residential yard/open space unless explicitly added later.

## Design intent

Make the garden feel complete and generous, not estate-scale and not tiny.

It should be:
- large enough to grow the entire requested plant palette properly
- compact enough for one household to maintain
- visually designed rather than sprawling
- large enough for real crop rotation and permanent beds
- large enough to include an enclosed greenhouse
- small enough that the daily route stays efficient

## Internal garden rooms

### Room 1 — entrance / greenhouse / work hub
Place the greenhouse close to the primary garden entrance and convenient water/electricity if feasible.

Adjacent features:
- potting/work area
- hose/irrigation header
- small tool storage or staging
- seedling hardening area
- attractive entrance marker

### Room 2 — kitchen garden
The most frequently visited beds.

Primary crops:
- tomatoes
- bell peppers
- jalapeños
- eggplant
- English cucumber
- cabbage
- basil
- parsley
- cilantro

Use efficient 30–48 in paths, strong trellises, and drip irrigation.

### Room 3 — permanent edible garden
- asparagus
- rhubarb
- selected thyme/rosemary/lavender in biologically correct dry areas

Permanent beds should sit outside the annual crop rotation but within convenient walking distance.

### Room 4 — cutting / flower garden
- dahlias
- zinnias
- cosmos
- snapdragons
- marigolds
- coneflower
- black-eyed Susan
- daisies/daylilies/geraniums as resolved

Use flower masses rather than isolated plants.

### Room 5 — rose structure
Classic red roses act as architectural punctuation rather than consuming a giant standalone rose garden.

Use:
- entrance pair/group
- path-end focal grouping
- flower-garden transition

Optional white rose remains one backyard-only exception.

### Room 6 — flexible/sprawling crop zone
- pumpkin
- squash
- potatoes
- garlic

Place at an outer edge so vines can move into designated space without crossing the central path system.

### Room 7 — shade pocket
For impatiens and any plant that genuinely prefers partial shade.

Do not force this room if the quarter-acre site is uniformly exposed; use shade created by a suitable structure or nearby landscape where biologically appropriate.

### Room 8 — wildflower tub / pollinator detail
The requested wildflower feature remains a dedicated tub, not the whole quarter acre.

It can sit near a bench/path junction or garden edge where it is visible and easy to observe.

## Path model

### Primary spine
~4 ft wide where practical.
Connect:
entrance → greenhouse → kitchen garden → flower/perennial rooms → outer flex area.

### Secondary paths
~2.5–3 ft as appropriate for bed access.

Avoid excessive path area, but never make maintenance dependent on stepping into bed soil.

## Bed model

Default to a hybrid rather than making every square foot raised beds.

Use raised beds selectively for:
- drainage problems
- high-frequency crops
- accessibility
- soil-control needs
- polished visual areas near the entrance/greenhouse

Use in-ground beds/rows where they are more economical for:
- potatoes
- pumpkin/squash
- cutting flowers
- larger rotation blocks

## Maintenance expectation

A quarter-acre mixed garden is still substantial.

The design should target a manageable peak-season routine by:
- drip irrigation
- mulched paths/beds
- permanent trellises where practical
- concentrated crop groups
- limited lawn-edge complexity
- accessible compost/tool staging
- no high-maintenance crops scattered elsewhere on the 2-acre yard

## Hard boundary rule

The 2-acre yard is **context**, not the garden.

Unless the user later explicitly expands it, the Garden OS should design and budget within the approximately **10,890 sq ft quarter-acre envelope**.
