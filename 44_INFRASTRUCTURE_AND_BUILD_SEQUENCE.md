# Infrastructure + Build Sequence — v0.5

**Goal:** build the quarter-acre garden in the order that prevents rework.

This is not a shopping list that assumes every upgrade is necessary. It is a staged infrastructure plan. Site measurements, soil, wildlife, water flow, and greenhouse model determine final quantities.

## Build-order invariant

Do not install permanent beds, buried irrigation, greenhouse anchors, or fencing before the quarter-acre envelope and access route are confirmed.

Preferred sequence:

**SITE → WATER/DRAINAGE → GREENHOUSE → PRIMARY PATHS → PERMANENT BEDS → IRRIGATION → ANNUAL BEDS → TRELLISES → FLOWERS/ROSES → POLISH**

---

# Phase 0 — site lock

Before construction:
- stake a 90 × 121 ft default quarter-acre rectangle or equivalent 10,890 sq ft shape
- confirm it does not interfere with driveway, septic, utilities, mowing, trees, drainage, or normal yard use
- observe sun
- identify the north side
- locate water/electric feasibility
- mark wet/low spots

Walk the staked footprint for at least several days before committing.

## Exit gate
You can answer:
- where the entrance is
- where the greenhouse goes
- where water enters
- which side is north
- where primary paths run

---

# Phase 1 — greenhouse foundation and service hub

Reserve **16 × 24 ft** for the greenhouse plus adjacent hardening/work apron.

Before greenhouse purchase/assembly, resolve:
- exact model/anchoring method
- foundation/base requirement
- door location
- vent/fan layout
- snow/wind rating appropriate to manufacturer/local requirements
- water route
- electrical needs
- drainage around structure

## Electrical
Any greenhouse electrical work should use outdoor/wet-location appropriate equipment, GFCI protection where required, and code-compliant installation.

## Water
Provide a nearby shutoff and hose connection if practical.

## Work apron
Use a stable drained surface suitable for:
- potting
- seedling racks
- wheelbarrow
- soil/media bins

---

# Phase 2 — primary paths

## Main spine
Target ~4 ft clear width.

Must handle:
- wheelbarrow
- garden cart
- harvest baskets
- two people passing reasonably

## Secondary paths
Target ~30–36 in where practical.

## Surface hierarchy

### Low-cost
- compacted soil + heavy mulch/wood chips

### Durable middle
- compacted aggregate/fines for primary spine + mulch secondary paths

### Polished
- well-built gravel/stone primary axis with clean edging; mulch or gravel secondary paths

Avoid creating slick mud channels or surfaces that wash into beds.

---

# Phase 3 — compost / materials / wash staging

## Compost
Default residential setup:
- 2–3 bays or bins
- each roughly 3–4 ft square if using a bay system

Functions:
- active pile
- curing pile
- finished material / leaf reserve

Keep convenient but not in the main visual focal line.

## Soil/media storage
Near greenhouse:
- sealed/dry bins for seed-start mix
- potting mix
- labels/trays

Do not store fertilizer or pesticides where moisture/children/pets create risk.

## Harvest staging
Reserve a small table/bench or 4 × 8 ft apron near the garden entrance/greenhouse for:
- baskets
- weighing
- rough sorting
- hose rinse where appropriate

A full outdoor kitchen is unnecessary for the first build.

---

# Phase 4 — permanent crop beds

Build before annual beds because these locations should not move casually.

## Asparagus
- dedicated permanent bed
- full-sun/drained site
- weed-access plan

## Rhubarb
- separate crown positions
- do not share the dry-herb irrigation schedule

## Dry herbs
- thyme
- rosemary
- lavender

Use a drainage-favoring zone; raised/bermed treatment may be useful if native soil is too wet.

---

# Phase 5 — irrigation backbone

Do not buy emitter quantities until actual bed lengths are known.

## Minimum architecture
- water source
- backflow protection as appropriate
- shutoff
- filter
- pressure regulation where required by drip equipment
- main header
- individually controllable zones
- flushable ends

## Suggested zones
- `IRR-A`: tomatoes/peppers/eggplant
- `IRR-B`: cucumber/cabbage/kitchen herbs
- `IRR-C`: permanent asparagus/rhubarb
- `IRR-D`: dry herbs
- `IRR-E`: roses/flowers as needed
- `IRR-F`: greenhouse
- `IRR-G`: containers/wildflower tub

Pumpkin/squash/potato flex area may share a suitable annual zone if hydraulically practical.

## Design rule
Do not run hoses across the main path as a permanent condition.

# Phase 6 — annual bed modules

Use the modules in `43_2027_BED_ASSIGNMENT_AND_ROTATION.md`.

## Raised beds only where they earn their cost
Good candidates:
- high-frequency kitchen beds
- problem drainage
- accessible/polished entrance beds

## In-ground blocks
Best candidates:
- potatoes
- pumpkin/squash
- larger rotation blocks
- cutting flowers

---

# Phase 7 — trellis/support system

Install before vigorous growth.

## Tomatoes
Need support rated for mature plant + fruit + wind.

## Cucumber
One strong ~12 ft trellis zone.

## Morning glory
Dedicated ornamental support.

## Sweet peas
Separate cool-season support/netting.

## Dahlias
Stake/grid support early for taller cultivars.

## Visual rule
Use a limited material palette, for example:
- dark metal
- cedar
- galvanized steel

Do not mix six unrelated support styles unless hidden in production blocks.

---

# Phase 8 — fencing / wildlife exclusion only if justified

Do not automatically build expensive deer fencing without evidence.

## If deer pressure is confirmed
Solve structurally at garden-perimeter scale rather than protecting every plant individually.

## If rabbits are the main problem
Lower exclusion may be enough.

## If groundhogs are present
Design specifically for digging/climbing pressure.

Record actual pressure before selecting fence height/material.

---

# Phase 9 — rose + flower structure

After sun/airflow and paths are clear:
- place 3–5 red roses as focal structure
- build cutting-flower rows/blocks
- place perennial drifts
- reserve shade pocket only where shade actually exists
- position wildflower tub near a visible path/bench junction

Do not install ornamental masses that block greenhouse vents, irrigation valves, or crop access.

---

# Phase 10 — sensors / automation

## Essential monitoring
- outdoor rain gauge
- greenhouse min/max thermometer
- simple soil-moisture checks by hand/trowel

## Best-value automation
- irrigation timer with manual override
- greenhouse thermostat/exhaust control
- greenhouse circulation fans
- high/low temperature alert

## Advanced optional
- flow/leak sensor
- greenhouse humidity logging
- soil-moisture probes in representative zones
- weather-aware irrigation adjustment
- camera for greenhouse remote check

Automation must fail gracefully.

# Three finish levels

## ESSENTIAL
Functional greenhouse, paths, irrigation, supports, compost, labels.

## POLISHED
Coordinated trellises, crisp edges, gravel main spine, organized greenhouse apron, screened compost, attractive hose station.

## RIDICULOUSLY GOOD
Formal central axis, integrated rose focal points, warm low-voltage lighting, custom greenhouse/work details, beautiful label/signage system, concealed utilities, harvest station, bench/focal destination.

## Biological rule
Never choose a finish upgrade that reduces:
- sun
- drainage
- ventilation
- cart access
- harvest access
- greenhouse cooling

---

# Construction stop conditions

Pause before irreversible work if:
- water pools where greenhouse/bed would sit
- quarter-acre footprint is substantially shaded
- utility/septic conflict appears
- greenhouse would shade the main kitchen garden significantly
- access requires crossing driveway with permanent hose
- deer/groundhog evidence changes fence needs

A one-week delay before concrete/anchors/buried line is cheaper than years of working around a bad placement.
