# Rose + Flower Design System — v0.6

**Status:** DESIGN GEOMETRY DEFAULT / CULTIVARS PARTLY PROVISIONAL  
**Aesthetic:** formal bones + cottage abundance  
**Rose invariant:** red roses dominate; maximum one optional white rose in the backyard only.

This file converts the flower list into a composition rather than a collection of unrelated specimens.

## Core visual rule

Use:
- repetition
- blocks/drifts
- clear height layers
- a deliberate bloom sequence
- restrained focal points

Avoid:
- one plant of everything
- random rose placement
- tall flowers shading vegetables
- dense planting around rose canes
- an ornamental plan that creates disease or maintenance problems

---

# Rose strategy for humid Kentucky conditions

University of Kentucky identifies black spot as the most common and serious rose disease in Kentucky. UK guidance emphasizes cultivar resistance, airflow, morning sun where possible, sanitation, and avoiding routine overhead irrigation.

Sources maintained in the Research Ledger:
- https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/ppfs-or-w-10
- https://publications.ca.uky.edu/files/id118.pdf

## Important evidence caveat

Older Kentucky Pest News/Extension material includes cultivar resistance lists assembled from multi-state trials. These are useful historical candidates, **not current guarantees**. Black-spot resistance can vary by location and pathogen population, and rose rosette disease can affect highly disease-resistant roses too.

Therefore cultivar status is:
- `CANDIDATE` until current nursery availability and local performance are checked
- never labeled truly “disease proof”

# Default rose architecture — 3 to 5 red roses

## RS-ENT-L + RS-ENT-R — classic entrance pair

Role:
- the strongest formal element in the garden
- matched red roses on either side of the south entrance
- visually signal the garden before the visitor reaches the production beds

Preferred form:
- classic high-centered / hybrid-tea or grandiflora appearance
- repeat-blooming
- hardy through the Richmond 6b–7a working range
- documented black-spot tolerance/resistance weighted heavily

### Historical Kentucky research candidates for the classic-red role
These appear in older Kentucky resistance compilations and preserve the requested classic rose look:
- `Mister Lincoln`
- `Olympiad`
- `Alec's Red`
- `Chrysler Imperial`

**Current default shortlist:** `Mister Lincoln` and `Olympiad` receive the first availability/local-performance check because they match the classic red visual role. Neither is locked solely from historical lists.

## RS-FOCAL — path/flower transition rose

Use one red rose at the flower-room transition/path-end focal point.

Two valid strategies:

### Strategy A — all classic
Use the same cultivar as the entrance pair for strict repetition.

### Strategy B — disease-resilient landscape backup
Use a red landscape/shrub rose with stronger disease performance as a lower-maintenance structural plant while keeping the entrance pair classic.

Historical Kentucky/Tennessee regional research identified red shrub roses such as `Home Run` and `Knock Out` as strong disease-resistance candidates. Kentucky Nursery Crop Extension also describes Double Knock Out as broadly hardy and black-spot resistant.

This is a **maintenance option**, not permission to replace the requested classic rose look everywhere.

## Roses 4–5 — optional repetition

If five red roses are used:
- 2 entrance
- 1 focal
- 2 repeated along the flower-room edge or secondary transition

Do not create a dense five-rose clump. Give each mature plant airflow and access.

## Optional white rose

Maximum one.

Rules:
- backyard/inside garden only
- not part of the symmetrical entrance pair
- used as a surprise contrast
- same disease-resistance and hardiness standard as red roses

Older UK resistance lists include `Polarstern` among white/light hybrid-tea candidates, but it remains a historical candidate rather than a lock.

# Rose siting + care geometry

For each rose reserve:
- mature-width clearance from path edge
- room to prune from all sides
- no dense groundcover against the crown/canes
- drip or soil-level watering
- good air movement
- strong sun, with morning sun especially valuable for drying foliage

## Rose exclusion zone

Do not install permanent obstructions, irrigation manifolds, or tight edging so close that pruning and cleanup become awkward.

## Rose-rosette watch

Because rose rosette can affect cultivated roses regardless of normal black-spot resistance:
- learn abnormal witches'-broom / excessive thorn / distorted-growth symptoms
- photograph suspicious growth
- escalate to Extension/diagnostic confirmation rather than repeatedly spraying fungicide for a viral disease

# Flower room composition

## FL01 — dahlia block
Default: **8–12 plants**.

Composition:
- 2–3 staggered rows depending mature cultivar width
- tallest types toward north/back
- stake/support at planting or early growth
- maintain a harvest lane

Role:
- dramatic summer/fall flowers
- bouquets
- late-season focal mass

Do not mix so many cultivars that tuber labeling becomes impossible. A restrained 3–5 cultivar palette is easier to identify and repeat.

## FL02 — zinnia + cosmos cutting block

### Zinnias
Default visual target: approximately **24–36 plants** across one or two successions, adjusted for cultivar spacing.

Role:
- dense saturated color body
- high-frequency cutting
- pollinator use

### Cosmos
Default target: approximately **10–16 plants**.

Role:
- airy movement above/behind zinnias
- do not overfertilize into floppy foliage

### Marigolds
Default target: approximately **18–30 plants** used mainly as repeated edge/punctuation groups.

Do not scatter one marigold beside every vegetable based on companion-plant folklore.

---

# FL03 — perennial drift

The perennial block should read as a few repeated masses.

Default first-year planting envelope:
- purple coneflower — 5–7
- black-eyed Susan — 7–9
- daylilies — 5–7
- daisies — 3–5 once species is resolved
- hardy geranium — 3–5 only if the requested geranium is resolved as perennial `Geranium`

Arrange in irregular drifts, not checkerboard rows.

## Height rhythm

Back/taller:
- selected daylilies
- taller Rudbeckia/daisy types

Middle:
- coneflowers
- black-eyed Susans

Front/ground layer:
- hardy geranium if selected
- compact annual edge color

# Cool-season flower sequence

## Snapdragons
Default first-year target: **18–24 plants** in one or two grouped rows.

Use:
- early bouquet production
- strong vertical spikes
- repeat near the entrance or cutting block rather than scattering

## Sweet peas
Use one dedicated **8–12 ft support/trellis**.

Default:
- sow/plant enough for a continuous but uncrowded trellis face
- harvest flowers frequently
- clearly label as ornamental/non-edible

Do not use the morning-glory trellis for sweet peas at the same time unless the seasonal transition is intentionally managed.

---

# Warm-season vertical flower

## Morning glory
One dedicated arch, obelisk, or trellis.

Default:
- 2–4 vines depending structure width/cultivar vigor
- monitor reseeding
- never let it escape onto tomatoes, cucumber, roses, gutters, utility equipment, or greenhouse vents

---

# Conditional flowers

## Lupines
Treat as a **trial crop**, not a guaranteed permanent drift, because Richmond heat/humidity may not favor every lupine species/cultivar.

Default trial:
- 3–5 plants
- excellent drainage
- observe survival through summer before expanding

## Geraniums
The requested word remains taxonomically unresolved.

If annual/bedding `Pelargonium`:
- 6–9 plants/containers as repeated seasonal color
- greenhouse can provide spring staging or overwintering experiment space

If hardy perennial `Geranium`:
- 3–5 plants in the perennial drift
- choose cultivar by mature spread and sun tolerance

## Daisies
Default design placeholder is Shasta-daisy-like perennial form, but the species remains unresolved until deliberately chosen.

---

# Shade pocket

## Impatiens
Do not fabricate a shade bed in full sun.

When a real part-shade pocket is found:
- use approximately 12–18 plants as one mass
- place near a path/greenhouse/structure where the shade is real
- keep even moisture without chronic saturation

If no useful shade exists, containers with controllable placement are preferable to forcing the crop into the sun.

# Bloom sequence target

## Spring / cool season
- sweet peas
- snapdragons
- early rose flush as established

## Early summer
- roses
- daylilies
- first zinnias/marigolds

## High summer
- zinnias
- cosmos
- dahlias
- coneflowers
- black-eyed Susans
- morning glory

## Late summer / fall
- dahlias
- zinnias
- coneflower/Rudbeckia seed-head structure
- selected cosmos
- roses if reblooming

The wildflower tub supplies a separate looser ecological detail and should not be mixed into the formal flower-block accounting.

# Color strategy

The only hard flower-color rule is the rose rule: **red roses dominate**.

Recommended overall composition:
- red rose anchors
- purple/lavender coneflower/lavender accents
- yellow/gold Rudbeckia/marigolds
- broad zinnia/dahlia bouquet colors
- restrained white accents only where they improve contrast

Avoid forcing every flower into a red-only palette; that would flatten the abundant cottage character.

---

# Flower maintenance simplification

Every flower group receives one primary reason to exist:
- roses — architecture/focal
- dahlias — drama + cutting
- zinnias — cutting/color workhorse
- cosmos — air/movement
- coneflower/Rudbeckia — perennial pollinator backbone
- snapdragon/sweet pea — cool-season cutting
- marigold — front-edge repetition
- morning glory — vertical summer feature
- impatiens — shade color

If a planting has no clear role, do not add more of it simply to fill soil.
