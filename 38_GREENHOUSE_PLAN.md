# Enclosed Greenhouse Plan — Richmond, Kentucky

**Status:** DESIGN SIZE LOCKED / EXACT SITING PROVISIONAL  
**Updated:** 2026-09-05

The quarter-acre garden includes a **real enclosed greenhouse inside the garden footprint**, not merely a temporary row cover or open high tunnel.

## Default size

**16 ft × 24 ft = 384 sq ft**

This is deliberately a substantial residential greenhouse: large enough for propagation, season extension, protected crops, potting, and overwintering experiments without turning the project into commercial greenhouse production.

The greenhouse counts inside the quarter-acre garden envelope, not in addition to it.

## Primary jobs

1. start tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, herbs, and flowers earlier
2. harden and stage transplants before outdoor planting
3. extend spring and fall harvests
4. grow cool-season greens/herbs beyond normal outdoor windows
5. provide protected space for selected tomatoes/peppers/cucumbers if useful
6. overwinter tender plants where temperature capability allows
7. store active propagation supplies, trays, pots, labels, and small tools
8. serve as the garden's weatherproof work room

## Not the default purpose

Do not heat the structure all winter simply because it can be heated. Full winter heating is an energy/cost decision and should be crop-driven.

University of Kentucky greenhouse guidance emphasizes that Kentucky greenhouses may need heat for early spring/late fall production and substantial ventilation/cooling during hot humid summers. The design therefore prioritizes ventilation before luxury automation.

## Structure baseline

Preferred residential specification:
- rigid framed greenhouse
- durable glazing such as twin-wall polycarbonate or equivalent long-life greenhouse glazing
- minimum useful sidewall height around 6.5–7 ft; taller is welcome
- full-height end door wide enough for cart/wheelbarrow access
- strong anchoring for Kentucky storms
- gutters where practical for rainwater collection
- electrical service only if safely installed to code
- freeze-protected water access if year-round use is intended

## Orientation

Richmond is south of 40° N. UK educational greenhouse guidance notes that north–south orientation can favor warm-weather light distribution and ventilation in locations south of 40° latitude, while winter-light goals can change the tradeoff.

Therefore:
- **default candidate:** long axis north–south
- final orientation must also account for shade, prevailing wind, entrance/path geometry, and winter-use goals
- do not lock orientation until the actual quarter-acre rectangle is placed on the property

## Ventilation and summer control

Kentucky heat/humidity makes this mandatory.

Minimum design intent:
- operable roof and/or upper vents
- large doors/end openings
- side ventilation where structure permits
- circulation fan(s)
- thermostatic exhaust fan if natural venting is insufficient
- removable shade cloth for peak summer

UK guidance specifically notes that greenhouse cooling in Kentucky commonly relies on air exchange and shading, and that evaporative cooling is less effective in humid conditions.

## Interior layout — 16 × 24 ft

### Center aisle
- ~3–4 ft clear main aisle
- keep it cart/wheelbarrow friendly

### Bench zone
- one or two 30–36 in deep benches along long walls where light allows
- seed trays, transplants, herbs, propagation

### Ground/large-container zone
Reserve at least one section without a permanent bench for:
- tall tomatoes
- cucumbers
- peppers
- large pots
- temporary overwintering plants

### Potting station
A compact bench near the entrance for:
- media
- labels
- hand tools
- trays
- fertilizer/measurement supplies

### Water
- hose connection or indoor line if practical
- floor designed so spills/drainage are harmless
- avoid standing water

## Heating levels

### Level 0 — unheated season extension
Use solar gain + frost cloth/thermal mass where useful.

### Level 1 — frost protection
Small controlled heater sized only to protect plants during cold events.

### Level 2 — actively heated production
Only if winter/very-early crops justify the energy and reliability burden.

If heat is installed, loss-of-heat contingency matters because a single cold night can destroy protected crops.

## Crop allocation

### Priority greenhouse residents
- seedlings/transplants for the whole garden
- early tomatoes/peppers/eggplant
- basil and herbs during shoulder seasons
- snapdragon/sweet-pea starts as timing requires
- dahlia propagation/startup if used
- cool-season greens as future additions if desired

### Optional protected production
- one or more indeterminate tomatoes trained vertically
- English cucumber
- selected peppers

Do not fill the greenhouse permanently with crops that make seed-starting impossible.

## Biosecurity / disease workflow

The greenhouse can amplify disease if crowded.

Rules:
- quarantine questionable new plants when practical
- remove diseased debris promptly
- sanitize reusable trays/tools between propagation cycles
- maintain airflow between plants
- avoid chronically wet foliage
- scout undersides of leaves
- maintain separate greenhouse observation records

## Greenhouse zones in the Garden OS

`GH-PROP` — propagation/seedlings  
`GH-BENCH` — pots/herbs/young plants  
`GH-TALL` — tall protected crops  
`GH-WORK` — potting/work/storage  
`GH-QUAR` — temporary quarantine/observation area

## Greenhouse record set

Track:
- inside daily min/max temperature during active seasons
- humidity if sensor added
- sowing dates
- germination
- pot-up dates
- pest/disease events
- heater/fan failures
- transplant movement in/out
- crop yields if fruiting crops are grown inside

## Exact siting gate

Final greenhouse coordinates require:
- quarter-acre garden location
- north
- shade objects
- prevailing exposure
- water route
- electrical feasibility if used
- drainage
- path/door approach

The greenhouse should be easy to reach from the house and should not cast avoidable shade over the main production beds.
