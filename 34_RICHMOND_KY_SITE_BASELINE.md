Reference:  
https://madison.mgcafe.uky.edu/sites/madison.ca.uky.edu/files/Taking%20Soil%20tests%202018.pdf

For this project, do **not** composite the entire two-acre yard. Only test management zones that matter to the quarter-acre garden.

Suggested sample IDs:

## Greenhouse research baseline

University of Kentucky greenhouse guidance is now part of the project evidence base.

Key implications for Richmond:
- greenhouse ≠ high tunnel; the requested structure is a real enclosed controlled-environment greenhouse
- heat may be needed for early/late production, but full winter heating is optional and costly
- ventilation/cooling is critical in Kentucky's hot humid summers
- passive vents, large openings, fans, and shade are higher-priority infrastructure than decorative greenhouse accessories
- because Richmond is south of 40° N, a north–south long-axis orientation is a useful default candidate, but actual shade/wind/access can override it

See `38_GREENHOUSE_PLAN.md` and research entries R-026 through R-029.

## Frost and topography

Within the 2-acre yard, identify:
- cold low basins
- exposed high points
- warm south-facing areas
- cooler north-facing areas
- wind corridors
- protected masonry pockets

The quarter-acre garden should avoid the coldest/wettest part of the yard if a better full-sun site exists.

## Humidity / disease implications

Structural prevention:
- generous spacing
- full/morning sun where practical
- soil-level irrigation
- mulch to reduce splash
- strong greenhouse and outdoor airflow
- resistant cultivars where they preserve the requested type
- crop-family history

Highest-priority disease monitoring:
- tomatoes
- peppers
- cucurbits
- roses
- greenhouse seedlings/crowded protected crops

## Water reality

Only the quarter-acre garden needs a dedicated garden irrigation plan.

Before location is locked, measure:
- distance from water source to quarter-acre entrance
- distance to greenhouse
- pressure/flow if drip expands
- elevation difference
- whether any path/drive crossing complicates routing

A garden that is easy to water will be maintained better than an equally sunny garden that creates daily hose friction.
