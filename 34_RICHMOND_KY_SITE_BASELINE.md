## Hardiness

### Primary authority
USDA 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map:  
https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/

USDA hardiness zones describe average annual extreme minimum winter temperature. They are useful for perennial survival and are **not** a complete planting calendar.

### Richmond working interpretation
Current city/ZIP-level map summaries place Richmond/Madison County across **Zones 6b and 7a**.

Working rule:
- permanent purchases should be comfortable in 6b where practical until the parcel is pinned on the USDA interactive map
- a south-facing protected wall may behave warmer than an exposed low field
- a low frost pocket may behave colder than the mapped zone suggests

## Soil testing — major two-acre advantage

Madison County currently advertises free soil testing for residents, with a **10-sample limit per home/farm per calendar year**.

Reference:  
https://madison.mgcafe.uky.edu/sites/madison.ca.uky.edu/files/Taking%20Soil%20tests%202018.pdf

### Do not submit one giant two-acre composite sample

That would hide important differences.

Recommended sample IDs:
- `SOIL-A` primary vegetable/kitchen-garden candidate
- `SOIL-B` alternate vegetable/rotation block
- `SOIL-C` asparagus/rhubarb permanent bed
- `SOIL-D` rose/cutting-flower area
- `SOIL-E` lavender/rosemary/thyme dry-zone candidate
- `SOIL-F` visibly wet/poor/drainage-problem area
- `SOIL-G` future expansion area if needed

Use additional samples only when a distinct management zone exists.

## Kentucky planting-date authority

University of Kentucky Extension `ID-128 — Home Vegetable Gardening in Kentucky`:  
https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/id-128

Current Kentucky tables provide regional earliest/latest safe planting dates. For planning, Richmond is handled using Central/Eastern Kentucky guidance, then adjusted from actual parcel microclimate and weather.

Examples relevant to the requested garden from current Kentucky guidance include approximately:
- asparagus crowns — March establishment window
- rhubarb crowns — March establishment window
- spring cabbage — late March/early April depending regional line
- cucumbers — around early May Central, later toward Eastern guidance
- tomatoes — around early May Central, later toward Eastern guidance
- eggplant — early/mid May
- pumpkins — early May
- summer/winter squash — early/mid May
- garlic — fall, with dedicated UK guidance emphasizing roughly October into early November

These are planning anchors, not guarantees.

## Frost and topography rule

UK commercial/home guidance explicitly warns that small topographic differences can materially affect frost timing.

On a two-acre property, identify:
- low basins where cold air settles
- exposed hilltops
- south-facing warm slopes
- north-facing cool slopes
- warm masonry/wall pockets
- wind corridors

Do not put the earliest warm-season planting in the coldest low point simply because it receives sun.

## Humidity / disease implications

Richmond's warm-season garden should be designed for Kentucky's humid disease pressure.

Structural prevention:
- generous spacing
- morning/full sun where practical
- soil-level irrigation
- mulch to reduce soil splash
- no unnecessary leaf wetting
- remove confirmed diseased material appropriately
- maintain crop-family history
- choose resistant cultivars where they preserve the requested type

Highest-priority disease monitoring groups:
- tomatoes
- peppers
- cucurbits
- roses

## Two-acre water reality

A two-acre yard can exceed convenient hose reach very quickly.

Before beds are locked, measure:
- water-source location
- static/dynamic pressure if irrigation expands
- practical hose distance
- elevation change to candidate garden core
- whether a buried mainline or remote spigot would reduce daily labor

The garden core should be chosen partly by **water friction**, not only by sunlight.

## Wind / exposure

Before locating tomatoes, dahlias, tall cosmos, climbing vines, and rose focal points:
- observe prevailing storm/wind direction
- map unprotected open-yard exposure
- identify fences/buildings that create turbulence or shelter

Trellis engineering belongs in the site plan, not as an afterthought after plants are six feet tall.
