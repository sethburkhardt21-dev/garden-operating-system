# Greenhouse Operating System — v0.5

**Structure:** 16 × 24 ft enclosed greenhouse  
**Location:** inside the quarter-acre Richmond garden  
**Status:** OPERATING LOGIC LOCKED / FINAL EQUIPMENT SIZING SITE-DEPENDENT

The greenhouse is not just a structure. It needs a repeatable operating system so it improves the garden instead of becoming a hot, crowded storage shed.

## Greenhouse mission hierarchy

Priority order:
1. propagation for the outdoor garden
2. transplant staging and hardening support
3. shoulder-season production
4. selected protected crops
5. overwintering experiments where practical
6. weatherproof garden work space

If these roles conflict, propagation capacity wins during late winter and spring.

---

# Interior zones

## GH-PROP — propagation bench
Use for:
- seed trays
- heat mats where needed
- humidity domes only during germination when appropriate
- propagation lighting if natural light is insufficient

Rule: seedlings graduate out of GH-PROP as soon as they no longer need propagation conditions.

## GH-BENCH — general bench
Use for:
- potted herbs
- recently potted-up seedlings
- small perennials
- temporary nursery plants

## GH-TALL — tall crop bay
Keep one floor-level bay free of permanent benching for:
- 1–2 protected tomatoes
- English cucumber
- selected peppers
- large containers

Do not turn the entire greenhouse into permanent tall-crop production before spring seed starting is complete.

## GH-WORK — potting/work zone
Near the entrance:
- potting surface
- labels
- hand tools
- media bins
- trays
- scale/measuring tools
- cleaning supplies

## GH-QUAR — quarantine/observation
A small separable area for newly purchased or suspicious plants.

No new plant should be placed directly among valuable propagation stock when obvious pests or disease are present.

# Default environmental control logic

These are **engineering defaults**, not universal crop-optimum temperatures. Crop-specific requirements override them.

## Warm-weather ventilation sequence

### Stage 0 — normal
- vents/doors adjusted for current weather
- circulation fans available during active growing

### Stage 1 — ventilation demand
Around **75°F greenhouse air temperature**, begin checking whether natural ventilation is keeping pace.

### Stage 2 — active cooling
Around **80°F**, increase vent opening / fan exchange as needed for crops present.

### Stage 3 — high-heat mitigation
Around **85°F**, use the full summer strategy where needed:
- doors/end openings
- roof/side vents
- exhaust fan
- circulation fans
- shade cloth

### High-temperature alert
Around **90°F+**, treat the situation as an active stress event for most propagation/transplant work. Confirm:
- fan actually running
- vents physically open
- shade deployed if appropriate
- plants are not dry
- no controller/power failure

Do not use misting as a substitute for ventilation in a humid Kentucky greenhouse.

## Cold-weather modes

### Mode A — unheated extension
Use only crops tolerant of the expected lows.

### Mode B — frost protection
When tender material is present, a default frost-protection setpoint in the neighborhood of **38–42°F** can provide margin above freezing. Tune to the actual crop and heater capability.

### Mode C — actively heated production
Set temperature according to crop requirements and economics. Do not use one generic winter setpoint for tomatoes, seedlings, herbs, and dormant plants.

## Cold alert logic

When tender crops are present:
- warning if greenhouse approaches ~40°F and falling
- urgent if heater is expected to be active but temperature continues toward freezing
- verify power, fuel, thermostat, air circulation, and door/vent closure

A backup thermometer independent of the primary controller is strongly preferred.

# Humidity / condensation logic

Do not obsess over one perfect RH number. Instead prevent prolonged leaf wetness and stagnant condensation.

Escalate ventilation/air movement when:
- glazing remains wet for long periods
- foliage stays wet overnight
- dense seedlings are touching
- disease appears in propagation trays
- humidity remains very high while temperatures are cool

Default high-humidity alert for an occupied greenhouse can be set around **85% RH**, but use it as a prompt to inspect condensation and airflow, not as proof of disease.

# Water system

## Propagation
Use controlled watering rather than soaking every tray on a fixed schedule.

Check:
- cell weight
- media surface
- root stage
- tray drainage

## Benches / containers
Use:
- watering wand
- micro-drip where worthwhile
- saucers only where standing water will not create root problems

## Tall crop bay
Drip irrigation is preferred for fruiting crops.

### Water invariant
Elapsed time is not proof that a greenhouse crop needs water.

Greenhouses can dry containers quickly on sunny days even when outdoor beds are wet.

---

# Lighting

Natural light is primary.

Supplemental lighting is justified when:
- propagation begins during low-light winter conditions
- seedlings stretch despite good temperature/spacing
- a specific crop schedule requires reliable photoperiod/light intensity

Do not install large grow-light capacity simply because the greenhouse has electricity.

## Light placement rule

Any fixtures, shelves, hanging baskets, or opaque storage must not unnecessarily shade propagation benches.

---

# Summer operating mode

The greenhouse may become less valuable for ordinary cool-season propagation in midsummer unless actively cooled.

Recommended summer roles:
- selected protected tomatoes/cucumber/peppers
- propagation only when needed
- drying/weatherproof work
- nursery staging

Summer checklist:
- shade cloth available
- exhaust/ventilation tested
- circulation fans clean
- irrigation checked
- doors secured against wind
- insect screens inspected without restricting airflow excessively

---

# Winter operating mode

Default philosophy: **do not heat empty space.**

Possible winter uses:
- hardy greens/herbs under limited protection
- dormant tender material if compatible with temperature
- protected propagation start as spring approaches
- storage of clean active garden equipment that will not block growing space

Before committing to winter heat, estimate:
- required inside temperature
- expected outside low
- glazing/insulation
- heater capacity
- power/fuel cost
- failure consequences

---

# Spring propagation bottleneck management

Peak greenhouse demand occurs roughly February–May.

Create a tray-capacity ledger:
- `PROP-A` warm-season vegetables
- `PROP-B` cool-season vegetables
- `PROP-C` flowers
- `PROP-D` herbs/perennials

Every tray gets:
- crop/cultivar
- sow date
- cell size
- expected pot-up date
- expected greenhouse exit date

Do not start more trays than the greenhouse can physically hold after pot-up.

---

# Hardening workflow

Greenhouse-grown plants are not automatically hardened for outdoor wind/sun/cold.

Use the adjacent hardening apron:
1. begin with sheltered outdoor exposure
2. increase sun/wind duration gradually
3. protect from unexpectedly cold nights
4. reduce greenhouse-style pampering gradually
5. delay transplanting if forecast conditions are poor

Record hardening start for major crops.

# Pest and disease biosecurity

High-priority greenhouse pests:
- aphids
- whiteflies
- spider mites
- fungus gnats
- thrips

High-priority disease conditions:
- damping-off
- Botrytis/gray mold
- mildews where conditions permit
- root rots from saturated media

Weekly greenhouse scout:
- leaf undersides
- growing points
- sticky cards if used
- media surface
- stem bases
- condensation hotspots

## Sanitation
- wash/disinfect reusable trays between major propagation cycles
- remove dead leaves
- keep hose ends off dirty floors
- do not reuse contaminated media blindly
- isolate suspicious plants

# Failure playbooks

## Power failure — hot weather
**FIRST 5 MIN**
- open manual doors/vents
- confirm plants have water
- deploy shade if needed

**NEXT 24H**
- restore ventilation control
- inspect for heat scorch/wilt

## Power/heater failure — freezing weather
**FIRST 5 MIN**
- close unnecessary openings
- deploy inner frost protection if available
- start backup heat if safely available

**NEXT 24H**
- repair primary heat
- document minimum temperature reached

## Fan failure
- open natural ventilation immediately if weather allows
- avoid assuming a thermostat display proves air exchange is happening

## Water failure
- prioritize propagation trays and small containers first
- use stored/emergency water where available

# Monitoring package

Minimum useful sensors:
- independent min/max thermometer
- greenhouse temperature sensor/controller
- outdoor reference temperature

Recommended:
- humidity sensor
- soil/media moisture only for representative critical crops
- power-loss notification if feasible

Optional advanced:
- vent-position sensor
- flow/leak monitoring
- camera for remote visual check

The greenhouse must remain operable manually if automation fails.

---

# Daily greenhouse brief

### URGENT
- overheating/freezing risk
- heater/fan/power failure
- dry seedling trays
- major pest/disease outbreak

### THIS WEEK
- pot-up seedlings
- move plants through hardening
- clean trays/benches
- adjust shade/ventilation for season

### OPTIONAL
- propagation experiment
- sensor tuning
- extra protected crop trial

### DO NOT TOUCH YET
- do not move seedlings to larger pots before roots/stage justify it
- do not start summer crops months early merely because space appears available
- do not heat the greenhouse to tropical conditions for no crop reason
