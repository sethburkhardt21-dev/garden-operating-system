# Master Layout — Provisional Architecture

This is **not yet a coordinate map**. It is the spatial logic that should survive once exact dimensions arrive.

## Zone A — Permanent edible bed
Reserve first. Do not place in annual rotation.
- asparagus
- rhubarb

Why: both are long-lived crops and become expensive to relocate.

## Zone B — Tall Solanaceae production
- heirloom tomato
- beefsteak tomato
- cherry tomato
- peppers
- eggplant

Rules:
- place tall tomatoes so they do not shade shorter crops
- give tomatoes independent vertical support
- do not use plant count to the point airflow collapses
- keep a documented disease/rotation history because tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, and potatoes share Solanaceae disease risks

## Zone C — Cucurbit vertical/edge zone
- English cucumber on trellis
- squash according to resolved growth habit
- pumpkin along an outside edge or dedicated run

Critical design point: pumpkin cannot be treated like a compact bed vegetable. Its vine path must be designed before planting.

## Zone D — Potato zone
- baby-harvest potatoes
- golden potatoes

Potential best fit:
- dedicated beds or large grow bags/containers if annual bed area is constrained
- keep rotation history with other Solanaceae

## Zone E — Allium / shoulder-season zone
- garlic

Reserve autumn-through-early-summer occupancy. Do not promise this bed to a spring crop that needs the same footprint before garlic harvest.

## Zone F — Culinary herb split hydrozone
**Moister group:** basil, parsley, cilantro  
**Dryer/drainage group:** rosemary, thyme, lavender

Do not put all herbs on one watering schedule simply because they are “herbs.”

## Zone G — Rose focal points
- classic red roses repeated intentionally
- optional single white rose only in backyard

Requirements:
- full sun where possible
- airflow
- splash reduction
- reachable for deadheading/pruning
- no dense wet planting directly against canes

## Zone H — Sun flower/cutting border
- dahlias
- zinnias
- marigolds
- coneflowers
- black-eyed Susans
- cosmos
- snapdragons
- selected daisies
- daylilies
- lupines only if cultivar/site proves suitable

Height strategy:  
back/tall → dahlias/cosmos/tall zinnias  
middle → coneflower/Rudbeckia/snapdragon/daylily  
front → marigolds/lower annuals

## Zone I — Shade/part-shade ornamental pocket
- impatiens
- some geranium selections depending exact type

Do not force impatiens into the hottest full-sun vegetable area.

## Zone J — Vertical flower architecture
Use separate trellises:
- morning glory = warm-season vigorous ornamental vine
- sweet pea = cool-season vine

Do not train both as though they share the same seasonal window.

## Zone K — Wildflower tub
Physically separate from the principal beds.
Goal: intentionally loose, colorful “jumble” with regional screening before seed choice.

## Access rule
No productive area should be designed so the gardener must step into compactable root-zone soil to harvest or maintain it.

## Final layout cannot be locked until
- dimensions
- north orientation
- shade map
- paths
- hose source
- permanent structures
are known.
