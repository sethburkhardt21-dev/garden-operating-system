## R-001 USDA Plant Hardiness
Source: USDA 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map
Use: perennial hardiness baseline
Finding: zone map is based on average annual extreme minimum winter temperature; it is not a complete microclimate model.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/

Finding: regional timing tables cover peppers, potatoes, pumpkins, rhubarb, fall-garden crops, etc.

## R-003 Garlic
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, Growing Your Own: Garlic (NEP-241)
Finding: Kentucky garlic is a fall-planted crop; Extension gives October–early November as the principal home-garden window and emphasizes sunny, well-drained soil.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/NEP241.pdf

## R-004 2026 garlic update
Source: UK Center for Crop Diversification, “Time to Order Garlic,” 2026-07-13
Finding: recommends ordering planting stock early; hardneck types are highlighted for Kentucky winter conditions; planting season spans roughly fall with local/variety variation.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://ccd.uky.edu/orderinggarlic

## R-006 Dahlias
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, Overwinter dahlia tubers with care
Finding: dahlias are not expected to overwinter reliably in Kentucky soil; tuber-storage planning is needed.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://exclusives.mgcafe.uky.edu/2020/hort/overwinter-dahlia-tubers-care

## R-007 Asparagus
Source: University of Kentucky Extension / Center for Crop Diversification
Finding: asparagus is a long-lived perennial vegetable; well-maintained plantings can remain productive for many years.

## R-008 Fall garden
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, “Plant now for a productive fall garden,” 2026-08-13
