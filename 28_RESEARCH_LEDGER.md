## R-001 USDA Plant Hardiness
Source: USDA 2023 Plant Hardiness Zone Map
Use: perennial hardiness baseline
Finding: zone map is based on average annual extreme minimum winter temperature; it is not a complete microclimate model.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://planthardiness.ars.usda.gov/

## R-002 Kentucky home vegetable garden timing
Source: University of Kentucky Cooperative Extension, Home Vegetable Gardening in Kentucky (ID-128)
Use: regional planting/calendar framework
Finding: current UK home-grower guidance uses this publication as the all-inclusive Kentucky vegetable-garden reference. The 2024 revision emphasizes planning, correct seasonal planting, disease/insect/weed management, and cultivar selection.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/id-128

## R-003 Garlic
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, Growing Your Own: Garlic (NEP-241)
Finding: Kentucky garlic is a fall-planted crop; Extension gives October–early November as the principal home-garden window and emphasizes sunny, well-drained soil.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/NEP241.pdf

## R-007 Asparagus
Source: University of Kentucky Extension / Center for Crop Diversification
Finding: asparagus is a long-lived perennial vegetable; well-maintained plantings can remain productive about 15 years. Drainage and weed control are important; first-year harvest should be avoided.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://ccd.uky.edu/resources/crops/vegetables/asparagus

## R-010 Kentucky summer watering
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, “Summer maintenance tips for a thriving vegetable garden,” 2026-07-09
Finding: Kentucky vegetables benefit from deep watering that wets roughly the upper 6–8 inches of soil; frequent shallow sprinkling encourages shallow roots.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://exclusives.mgcafe.uky.edu/2026/hort/summer-maintenance-tips-thriving-vegetable-garden

## R-011 Tomato disease pressure
Source: University of Kentucky Plant Pathology, Tomato “Blight” — Leaf Diseases that Cause Blighting in Home Gardens (PPFS-VG-38)
Finding: tomato leaf diseases are common in Kentucky home gardens; warm/wet early-summer weather increases fungal disease pressure.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://plantpathology.mgcafe.uky.edu/files/PPFS-VG-38.pdf

## R-012 2026 disease-resistant vegetable guidance
Source: Daviess County Extension Horticulture Winter 2026
Finding: early blight is identified as the most common tomato disease in Kentucky and often occurs with Septoria. The 2026 guidance names resistant/somewhat resistant tomato candidates including Matt's Wild Cherry, Mountain Magic, Sun Gold, Defiant, Mountain Fresh Plus, Mountain Merit and Stellar. It identifies bacterial spot as the most common pepper disease and recommends resistance to multiple races.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://daviess.mgcafe.uky.edu/newsletters/horticulture-winter-2026

## R-013 Vegetable cultivars for Kentucky
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, ID-133 Vegetable Cultivars for Kentucky Gardens
Finding: current UK home-grower pages still point gardeners to ID-133 for cultivar recommendations. Listed examples relevant here include Cherokee Purple, Big Beef, Mountain Magic, Sun Gold, Matt's Wild Cherry, Aristotle, Alliance, Red Knight and multiple squash/pumpkin types.
Caveat: cultivar table itself is older, so current disease guidance and current seed availability must be layered on top.
Status: RESEARCHED WITH AGE CAVEAT
URL: https://publications.mgcafe.uky.edu/files/id133.pdf

## R-014 Rhubarb
Source: University of Kentucky Extension, Growing Your Own: Rhubarb (NEP-242), issued 2024-12
Finding: Canada Red, MacDonald, Valentine and Victoria are recommended for Kentucky. One to three plants can be sufficient for a household; crowns are planted in early spring; full 8–10 week harvest is deferred until the third year; leaves are poisonous.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/NEP242.pdf

## R-015 Cucurbit IPM
Source: University of Kentucky, PPFS-VG-19 Sustainable Disease Management / Backyard Cucurbit IPM
Finding: cucumber beetles and squash bugs are monitored rather than automatically sprayed; the guidance gives approximately one insect per plant as a management trigger. Pollinator risk from pesticide use during bloom is specifically noted.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://publications.ca.uky.edu/sites/publications.ca.uky.edu/files/ppfs-vg-19.pdf

## R-016 Pumpkins
Source: University of Kentucky Center for Crop Diversification, Pumpkins
Finding: important late-summer/fall Kentucky pumpkin diseases include black rot, downy mildew, powdery mildew and Plectosporium; important pests include cucumber beetles, squash vine borer and squash bugs.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://ccd.uky.edu/resources/crops/vegetables/pumpkins

Source: GitHub `HapiCreative/jninty`, README inspected 2026-09-05

## R-019 OpenPlantDB
Source: GitHub `cwfrazier1/openplantdb`, README inspected 2026-09-05
Finding: CC0 machine-readable plant dataset with JSON/CSV, planting anchors, mature size, spacing, germination, maturity and PostgreSQL schema. Project explicitly warns that generic ranges vary by cultivar/region/microclimate.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://github.com/cwfrazier1/openplantdb

## R-020 Plant-it
Source: GitHub `MDeLuise/plant-it`, README inspected 2026-09-05
Finding: mature garden companion focused on care-event logging, photos and elapsed-time reminders; intentionally does not claim that reminders prove a plant biologically needs water/fertilizer. Active feature development has slowed.
Status: RESEARCHED
URL: https://github.com/MDeLuise/plant-it
