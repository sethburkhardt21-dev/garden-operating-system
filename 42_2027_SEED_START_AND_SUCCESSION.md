# 2027 Seed-Starting + Succession Calendar — Richmond, Kentucky

**Status:** LOCATION-CONFIRMED / WEATHER-ADJUSTABLE DEFAULT  
**Greenhouse:** 16 × 24 ft enclosed structure  
**Rule:** use this as the default 2027 production calendar, then move dates when real weather, soil temperature, plant maturity, or cultivar instructions justify it.

This schedule is intentionally practical. It does not attempt to start every requested plant from seed indoors. Some plants are better purchased as crowns/transplants/tubers or direct-sown.

## Working outdoor anchors

For a normal Richmond spring, use these planning targets rather than one magic frost date:
- **cool-season spring transplant window:** generally March into early April where crop/weather fit
- **warm-season transition:** generally early-to-mid May
- **fall garden establishment:** begins during summer, often much earlier than people expect
- **garlic:** fall planting, roughly October into early November under Kentucky guidance

The greenhouse gives flexibility, but it does not make cold outdoor soil disappear.

# January 2027

## Greenhouse / indoor planning
- inventory seed
- test old seed where germination is questionable
- order final cultivars
- clean trays/cell packs
- check propagation lights/heat mats
- assign tray capacity

## Start late January only where justified
### Snapdragons
**Default sow:** Jan 25–Feb 15  
Reason: slow early development and value as a cool-season flower.

### Geraniums
Only if the requested geranium is resolved as seed-grown `Pelargonium`. Otherwise wait for cultivar/type decision.

### Lavender / perennial flowers
Do not bulk-start without confirming whether seed stratification is needed for the chosen species/cultivar. Winter sowing may be more appropriate.

---

# February

## Feb 1–15
### Spring cabbage
Start first spring batch for transplanting once outdoor conditions are suitable.

### Parsley
Start because germination/development can be slow.

### Optional perennial flower propagation
- coneflower
- black-eyed Susan
- daisies
- lupine

Only after species-specific seed treatment is known. For native/perennial species needing cold stratification, use winter-sowing/stratification rather than pretending greenhouse warmth solves dormancy.

## Feb 20–Mar 5
### Bell peppers
Start main batch.

### Jalapeños
Start main batch.

### Eggplant
Start main batch.

Use warm germination conditions, then strong light and airflow after emergence.

Do not start so early that giant root-bound plants are waiting for May.

---

# March

## Mar 10–25
### Tomatoes
Start:
- heirloom
- beefsteak
- cherry

Target healthy, compact transplants rather than maximum plant size.

### Basil — optional early batch
Start a small greenhouse batch late March if an early protected supply is desired.

### Marigolds
Start late March/early April for transplanting after frost risk.

### Dahlia tubers
Wake/start selected stored tubers under protection around late March/early April if early growth is desired, but do not let them outgrow the greenhouse before safe planting.

## Outdoor/permanent spring work
As soil/weather allow:
- asparagus crowns
- rhubarb crowns
- spring cabbage transplants
- potato bed preparation/planting according to local conditions

### Sweet peas
Treat as a cool-season vine. Direct sow or establish early according to cultivar and workable soil; do not schedule them like morning glories.

---

# April

## Apr 1–15
### Basil main batch
Start for May transplanting.

### Zinnias — optional transplant batch
Start a limited batch 3–5 weeks before expected planting, or direct sow later. Do not overfill greenhouse benches with zinnias that are easy to direct sow.

### Cosmos — optional transplant batch
Same principle: small early batch only if desired.

## Apr 10–20
### Morning glories
Start only if transplanting is preferred; otherwise direct sow after warm conditions arrive. Avoid root disturbance by using suitable containers.

## Apr 15–25
### English cucumbers
Start 2–3 plants only a few weeks before outdoor transplanting. Cucurbits dislike sitting root-bound.

### Summer squash — if selected
Start a small batch or direct sow outdoors later.

### Winter squash — if selected
Same.

### Pumpkins
Start only 2 planned plants/hills or direct sow outdoors when warm enough.

## Hardening begins
Late April into May, start hardening:
- tomatoes
- peppers
- eggplant
- basil
- warm-season flowers

Do not harden by suddenly putting greenhouse-grown plants into a full windy sunny day.

---

# May

## Early-to-mid May transition
Weather/soil permitting:
- transplant tomatoes
- transplant peppers
- transplant eggplant
- transplant English cucumber
- plant squash/pumpkin
- transplant basil
- transplant warm-season annual flowers
- install every support before plants demand it

## Direct sow options
- zinnias
- cosmos
- marigolds where direct sowing makes sense
- morning glory after warm conditions

## Greenhouse transition
As outdoor crops leave:
- reclaim propagation bench space
- decide whether GH-TALL receives 1 protected tomato and/or cucumber trial
- do not reflexively fill all empty greenhouse space

---

# June

## Succession sowing
### Basil
Start/seed a replacement batch if the first plants are aging or a longer harvest window is wanted.

### Cilantro
Only sow when conditions are cool enough to justify leaf production; in hot conditions, expect rapid bolting and use small experimental batches.

### Zinnias/cosmos
A later sowing can extend bloom beyond the first planting.

## Fall cabbage begins earlier than most beginners expect
Prepare seed/tray space for summer start dates rather than waiting until autumn.

---

# Late June–July

## Jun 25–Jul 10
### Fall cabbage
Start fall transplants.

Keep seedlings:
- watered
- well lit
- ventilated
- protected from excessive greenhouse heat

In midsummer, the greenhouse may be too hot for good cabbage transplants unless cooling is excellent. A cooler protected nursery location may outperform the greenhouse.

## Flowers
Continue succession sowing of quick annuals only where they have enough season left to matter.

---

# August

## Fall crop transition
Transplant fall cabbage based on plant maturity and weather.

Use shade/irrigation support during establishment if heat persists.

## Cilantro / parsley
As temperatures begin moderating, resume small succession sowings for fall use.

## Greenhouse
Begin planning fall-extension occupancy:
- herbs
- selected cool-season crops
- clean empty bench space

---

# September

## Garlic planning
- order next garlic stock if not already ordered
- map bed
- prep only according to soil-test/site needs

## Greenhouse shoulder season
Use for:
- basil extension
- parsley/cilantro protection
- selected cool-season experiments
- propagating divisions/cuttings if desired

## Flower seed saving / propagation records
Record successful annual flower cultivars before cleanup.

---

# October–early November

## Garlic
Plant named healthy cloves in the mapped garlic bed.

## Tender plants
Before frost:
- decide which rosemary/geranium/container plants are worth protecting
- move only healthy material into greenhouse/quarantine
- do not import pests blindly

## Dahlias
Tag before frost; lift/store after appropriate frost/top-growth sequence.

---

# November–December

## Greenhouse winter mode
Choose deliberately:
- unheated extension
- frost protection
- limited active production

Do not keep tender crops requiring expensive heat unless they have a clear value.

## 2028 seed review
Record:
- germination performance
- transplant timing
- greenhouse bottlenecks
- varieties started too early/late
- bench capacity peaks

# Greenhouse capacity rule

Before each sowing batch, ask:
1. How many cells now?
2. How much space after pot-up?
3. What date should they leave?
4. What other batch needs the same bench then?

A 72-cell tray that becomes 72 larger pots can consume several times its original footprint. Plan **post-germination space**, not just tray count.

# Succession philosophy

Use succession primarily for:
- basil
- cilantro
- parsley where useful
- cabbage spring/fall
- zinnias/cosmos
- cucumber only if first planting declines and season remains

Do not create endless sowing chores for crops that already produce continuously from one planting.
